The Premise

A cynical American club owner is reunited with his lost love and must decide wether to help her and her rebellion leading husband escape.

Essentials

This American guy named Richard (aka Rick) owns a really cool club in Casablanca in the year 1941. World War II is going wild and French Morocco is in a weird limbo state that isn't totally independent but slightly more so than actual France, known as Vichy France. The local French police chief is hosting a Nazi officer.

History 101

I'm not going to go into intense detail on this, but here are a few things you should know. After the collapse of the French regime early into World War II, a new puppet regime referred to as Vichy France was put in-place. This state then had direct colonial rule of French Morocco. Vichy therefore acted as a sort of buffer between the French colonies and Nazi Germany. French Morocco specifically had an unusual ruling structure with the Sultan as a sort of figure head and the resident-general the true leader of the state. I'll note that the region-general at the time was initially very upset with the downfall of France and didn't like the Nazis at all, but bent the knee in order to keep some simblance of control over his territory, importantly keeping it out of German and Spanish control. Vichy France still had an army, but Germany obviously couldn't trust their recently defeated enemies, so they stationed military officers throughout major cities in the colonies, such as Casablanca.

History 202

Casablanca was actually a relatively new city. In the early 1900s it had only a population of about 20,000. It was a sudden and drastic explosion in wealth and population that grew the city to about 250,000 by the mid 1930s. Also, the film gets this wrong, but the art style would have been a very modern art deco architecture with many contemporary European styles.

The 1942 Audience

The film actually came out in January 1943, but was filmed through the middle of 1942. Audiences would have only recently heard anything about the city of Casablanca after major headlines in the news, such as Project Torch, an amphibious invasion of Northern Africa by the Allied forces. While the war had raged on for years, it would have only been about one when Americans would have started their involvement. IDK what I'm rambling about the but the line
