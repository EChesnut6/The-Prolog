This critique contains spoilers for Perfect Blue.

Watched this movie after remembering something about Darron Aranofsky "stealing" or "plagarising" Black Swan entirely from this movie. I remember originally thinking, wow reall? He stole the ENTIRE thing? So I watched this. It's been a while since I saw Black Swan but obviously with that film in the back of my mind while watching this, it affected how I saw the movie. However, ultimately I just don't know how anyone could walk away from watch both and try to call Aranofsky a thief. His idea feels completely original and separate. I think what he mostly gets flack for is that he denies being influenced. However here are his exact words on the subject,

"Not really, there are similarities between the films, but it wasn’t influenced by it. It really came out of Swan Lake the Ballet, we wanted to dramatize the ballet, that’s why it’s kind of up here and down there, because ballet is big and small in lots of ways." — Aranofsky

I think from this it's clear he interpereted being influenced as deciding to create the script/story from this film. Aparently, he has also noted Satoshi Kon as an influential director before. I'll finish by saying I think Aranofsky is in the clear and doesn't deserve any hate. There are clearly similarites between the movies in story and style, but they are ultimately about different things and are done in different ways.

So anyways, I think the movie is great. It does a fantastic job of keeping you lost and confused while also giving just enough to let the individual come to their own understanding. In fact, I think it's fairly clear that there is one ending, or at least way of understanding the film. Now, I'm just spitballing so it's really not all that accurate, but it's like this. Rumi has Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID), she's the one that kills all of the people and has a mental split as Mima leaves the group. Rumi then uses the insane super fan Mr. Me-Mania do her bidding, so maybe he killed some guys too. I think Mima is going through a difficult transition and the whirwind that is film and acting is too much leaving her dissociating which is why she ends up so confused. I'm realizing I don't understand this movie as well as I thought. Mr. Me-Mania thinks he's talking to the real Mima, stalking her constantly. He has deep psychological issues and is obsessed with her. I think he buys all those magazines where she poses nude as a possesiveness type of thing. He sees her as his, which is implied from the beginning where he's looking at her with his hand holding her. I think he truly tries to rape her, the real Mima, and he gets murdered. Then I guess Rumi kills that other guy, I can't remember his name and stashes him there. Rumi is definitely psycho and gets that ugly ass dude to help her with stuff. Other than that I can't say what's for sure real or what.
