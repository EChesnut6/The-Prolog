These are two points that don't ultimately mean much, story-wise. However, I think that understanding some things like this will enhance your enjoyment of the film by about 2%.

Number 1

King Farouk was the penultimate pharoah of Egypt reigning from 1920-1965, which means this film came out near the end of his reign. He was widely known for his extravagant lifestyle including many many marriages and probably equally many affairs. This was a person who America, or at least parts of it, would have been aware of and heard about.

Number 2

Little Lord Fauntleroy was a classic childrens book written in 1886. This American classic would have been widely familiar with the audience of the film in the year 1960. The story follows a young boy as he desicovers that he is the soole heir to a wealthy British Earl, who through Fauntleroy's kindness and love softens the Earl's ice cold heart.

Drama, Romance, and Comedy. The three things that when combined can often create perfect films. This is what I'd say is nearly one of them.

As you can tell from the rating, this is one that I quite enjoy. I found the comedy to be well executed and surprisingly tastefull. This isn't my first Billy Wilder movie. While I haven't seen many of his other classics such as Some Like It Hot, I have seen Double Indemnity. Which I enjoyed that movie but in different ways than this one. I'm not sure what happened culturally, but Double Indemnity was pretty horribly sexist. This one is substantially less. For modern audiences the language and actions come off as much more understandable and realistic whereas Double Indemnity comes off strange language-wise. But anyways, I think that Wilder's writing style with it's fast paced and witty lines make this one a treat. I thought it was genuinely hilarious at times. It reminded me in some ways of old cartoons like Tom and Jerry and Looney Tunes with their mostly wholesome sense of comedy. I think it is highly benefited by a solid performance from Jack Lemmon. He portrays such a helpless naive man who you can't help but root for as he tries to people please. I say root for, but it's more of cheering for him to stop. You like him and hate him at the same time. It's like watching a baby stumble over itself and just thinking, if you just did the thing then you'd be better off! It's like watching a horror movie character run up the stairs to escape the killer. You hate what they are actively doing, but you like them and are cheering for them to stop making the same mistake over and over. Lemmon does a pretty good job. Fred MacMurray also kills it as the slimey upper management executive who you just hate. He's such a slimeball. Shirley MacLaine I thought did a great job overall. There are some parts where her performance is lacking, but she does a good job playing that character. It feels quite real too. We've met those people who are always hung up on the wrong person and just can't walk away. I also think that there is a palpable since of why Miss Kubelik can't leave Sheldrake for Baxter initially. Baxter is constantly spineless and weak, and despite having feelings for Miss Kubelik he can never be upfront about them and constantly puts Sheldrake on a peddalstool. I'm gonna start talking in circles. Baxter does make an advance and seems to be successful until he's stood up, but he immediately makes excuses for her. When he finds out she's going with Sheldrake, he's incredibly frustrated but rather than moving on as he probably should have he is still kinda hung up on her. And even worse, he supports Sheldrake and is wingmanning for him. It's one of the saddest things to see, a man who thinks so little of himself that he's basically cukcing himself to justify not being with the girl he likes. I think this part especially speaks to the issue of American society and culture with regards to wealth and job title as status symbols. Baxter initially is the lowly employee who can't get the girl not because of anything other than his job and position within the ocmpany (i'm syaing this is what Baxter believse) Then, once he finally reaches the job, he realizes that it doesn't make a difference. He gets that position and thinks, ok, now I can get the girl, but it doesn't magically happen, so he finally realized that he had been took and needed to change. It was only when he grew a spine and was confident that the girl finally wanted him. I'm writing this and just further understand the great writing. This movie is honestly quite Shakespearian in it's plot and setup. I think that's why it's so highly regarded. Well, that and the very humanly written characters. The comedy really succeeds from the humorous circumstances and misunderstandings of the characters as well as clever repeated phrases.
