The Evolution of Comedy in the 00s and 10s

By Eli Chesnut • 2026-06-26

Intro

I was watching one of my favorite comedies of all time, The Other Guys when I realized there was a strange uniqueness in the comedy.

The Beginnings

Comedy is one of the original genres and has been apart of film from it's origins. Modern comedy owes it all to the likes of Charlie Chaplin and Buster Keaton who paved the way not just for comedy but film. During this silent era, the comedy was nearly entirely physical setting the stage for what was expected in comedy films for generations to come.

This changed when sound was added and made verbal humor a new and exciting avenue, but don't think the physical left. In comedies such as The Apartment the verbal comedy took precedent but small quirks and acts of physical comedy persist throughout. And it might be even wiser to call The Apartment an exception, as shows and characters like The Three Stooges continuously used slap-stick comedy. During this more developmental era, comedies experiented greatly with verbal and physical comedy, coming up with new ways of intertwining them. Artist experimented and refined their craft often using one style over the other. A type of equilibrium was ultimately found with each artist and their particular style. Another factor also played a major part, the explicitness of the material. Audiences from the 1940s and 1950s expected very different topics and types of humor discussed than the audiences of 1990s and 2000s. As we move forward in time, the audience expected more crass and raunchy humor as a way to remain edgy and relevant.

The Crude Era: 2000s

Entering the 2000s, the crass and edgy humor was never higher. The humor was crude in every way and typically leaned more physical. From this era, we got classics like Superbad, Step Brothers, and Anchorman. These films relied heavily on physical gags, improvisational banter, and envelope-pushing vulgarity that mirrored the growing pains of early-internet culture and a desire for escapism in a post 9/11 world. When the going got tough from tragedy and financial uncertainty, the comedy world responded with harsh absurdity, mirroring the current climate.

The Meta Era: 2010s and Beyond

As the 2010s approached, a subtle transition began. Comedies started to blend the traditional crassness of the previous decade with a sharper, more self-aware wit. A prime example of this transitional sweet-spot is The Other Guys.

By the mid-2010s, comedy shifted fully into a meta-referential, self-aware era. Audiences became more receptive to dry, dialogue-driven, and genre-deconstructive humor.

For instance, The Lego Movie subverted commercialism and hero tropes with relentless speed and self-parody, while The Nice Guys revived the buddy-cop dynamic with a focus on character-driven, slapstick tragedy. In this era, the humor is less about shock value and more about subverting expectations.

Meta became a norm. It was expeceted of audiences to know the references and the tropes that previous generations built. As if everything before was just the build up, the 2010s comedies were the punchline.
