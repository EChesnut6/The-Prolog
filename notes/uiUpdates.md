1. Create an Immersive Hero Section
Right now, the page feels split into isolated components. To make it feel like a cohesive experience, let’s bring the film's "vibe" into the layout itself.

The Bleeding/Blurred Backdrop: Instead of a stark off-white background everywhere, take the movie poster and create a large, full-bleed header section. You can apply a heavy CSS blur (backdrop-filter: blur(40px); or a stretched, low-opacity version of the poster) behind the main content area. This instantly introduces the color palette of the specific movie into the page UI.

Layering: Let the movie poster slightly overlap the boundary between the header and the content below it to break up the rigid grid.

2. Upgrade the Typography & Metadata Grid
The technical details (Runtime, Genres, IMDB) are currently stacked in a tight, vertical list that feels a bit like a spreadsheet.

Horizontal Meta Line: Move the basic stats into a sleek horizontal line right under the title or tagline (e.g., 2001 • 136 min • Mystery, Romance, Sci-Fi).

The Tagline: The tagline (LoveHateDreams...) is a great detail, but the italicized gray font makes it look like body copy. Bump up its size, make it a bold accent color, or give it some letter-spacing to make it look like a stylized movie quote.

Score Badges: The "Enjoyment" and "Filmmaking" boxes look a little blocky. Consider making them pills with a very subtle background fill, or remove the border entirely and just use bold typography with your accent color to make the ratings pop.

3. Inject Visual Texture into the Content
The bottom half of the page is a massive block of uniform text.

Bold the First Paragraph (Drop Cap/Lead-in): Make the first sentence or two of "The Primer" slightly larger or a different weight to pull the reader in.

Add inline quote blocks: If you mention specific concepts (like the "remix vs. remake" idea), pull a key sentence out and format it as a large, stylized pull-quote in the middle of the text.

4. Sophisticated Color Accents
The deep red/crimson you chose for the ratings and text links works incredibly well against the cream background.

Contrast Accents: To make sure it doesn't feel monotonous, introduce a sharp, vibrant accent color only for interactive or high-focus elements (like that "trailer" link or a hover state). A pop of cyan blue would perfectly counter the deep crimson, feel very intentional, and match the specific cool tones in the Vanilla Sky artwork.

Subtle Gradients: Use soft, linear gradients that fade to transparent to separate major sections instead of hard borders or massive empty spaces.
