## Primer

#### The Essentials
It's Batman, what else do you need to know? I think something that makes this movie interesting is that it's the first major live action project for the character. There was the classic TV show in the 60s that had a movie come out, but the change in style and quality is just absurdly drastic. 

##### The gap
For some reason, maybe just general economic factors, could be the changing role of comics in general, but there was not a single live action piece of media in the 1970s or early 80s even for Batman. I didn't look for others, but I want to say the late 70s is when christopher reeves' Superman was coming out. I think understanding this gap can only give a brief glimpse into the mindset of how huge this project was. Obviously in the year 2026, our cup runneth over with super hero media, so it's interesting to think about a time period where this was not the case, and super heros in general were not the norm in the pop culture sphere. 

#### Cast and Crew
this movie would definitely have had it's doubters and did. None of the choices for the film really made sense, and once reason to doubt was that Keaton was not really a serious actor. Or at least not known as one. Many of his previous projects were comedies. 
This was pretty similar for Tim Burton, now we know he would have had the artwork and vision to demonstrate and show his talents, but for the mass audiences, he would have been a relatively unknown and weird choice. His only two feature films that anyone would know him for would be [[Pee-wee's Big Adventure]] and [[Beetlejuice]]. Both lean more towards comedy than action/adventure superhero flick. 
Then you have Jack Nicholson, who was a seasoned vet and possibly considered an early legend. He brings it too. I think without his performance you never see Batman on the big screen the same way, and you definitely don't get Ledger's performance.

#### Batman
I wonder what the hero's reputation was at the time of release. I know that it was during the mid 80s that they held an election on wether they kill Jason Todd or not, which is a crazy thing to vote on with the audience. The movie is dripping with campy sillyness that only works in musicals and tim burton's style. Today, I think many audiences love the dark realistic horror-adjacent, or maybe that's just me... Anywho, The style of a batman movie is just so incredibly different than back then. 

#### The late 80s
Hair Metal, dress up, and vividity, all at near highs in the late 80s and I think it shows. Joker has always had a bright style with purple suits and green hair, but Nicholson's Joker takes it up a few notches and throws in some over the top Prince music to go with it. WHile gotham keeps it's dark and gritty environment, the characters and events in it all have a bright and energetic tone.
