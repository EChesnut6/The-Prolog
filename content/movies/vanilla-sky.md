## Primer

##### Remix
I'll mention that this is actually a remake of the spanish movie titled "Open your eyes." However, the director Cameron Crowe considers his version more of a remix rather than a true remake. The director of the spanish version, Alejandro Amenábar, also considers them to be the same song sung by very different voices, one more operatic while the other is rock and roll. Another fun fact is that Penelope Cruz plays the same character, Sofia, in both. Does that make a big difference when watching? No. Is it interesting? Yes. 

##### Essentials
As for things you should know, this movie has a little sci-fi going on and is purposefully confusing. You will and should ask what is real while watching. The movie often jumps forwards and backwards in time showing glimpses of whats to come. For watching the [trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k09OX40NLUw) I would stop at 1:22. From this the essential plot that you should be aware of is clear, David (Tom Cruise) is a young wealthy guy with few connections to anyone. He has a casual relationship with Julie (Cameron Diaz), has a lot of money, nice apartment, and even a fancy job. However, it then gets flipped on his head when he meets Sofia (Penelope Cruz). There is much more to it than that, but I think this is the essential plot that you should be aware of going in. 

##### Keep in Mind
Something you should pay special attention to is the use of commercial music. The music was chosen specifically to make indirect comment on what was happening in the film itself. Some songs are also used in more of an ironic sense, being turned on their head in a way to be used in contrast. Using commercial and popular music in a nonconventional way.

##### From the Future
One thing that should be noted watching this in a modern since is the romance between Tom Cruise and Penelope Cruz. Tom Cruise was married to Nicole Kidman during the filming of this movie in 2000. They then separated in early 2001. Tom Cruise and Penelope Cruz then started to date subsequently. Was their acting out romance what led to Cruise and Kidman's divorce??? That's what the headlines of tabloids pushed as this film came out. Cruise and Cruz's relationship lasted about 3 years before breaking up due to conflicting and demanding schedules. I mention all of this not because it is very relevant to the story, but I find it an interesting point since someone like me who wasn't born would not have known this. In fact, I didn't until doing some light research on the film and it came up in an interview.

## Review

##### This review is incomplete and therefore shit
As you can tell by my ratings of this, I generally liked this movie. I found the hard switch between the moods to be interesting. I liked the character of David as a complex protagonist. 

The ending, and really the last 3rd, of the movie is where ideas thrive. That's not to say that the first 2/3rds don't have pleanty to analyze and go over, but relative the the final act, they lack discussion. 

There are two main ways to look at this, either what we see and are told are the absolute truth. David has now woken up 150 years later. He took the procedure and everything from the night of the club after is a dream that went wrong. Or, another popular theory is that everything after the crash is a dream in a coma state. 

Personally, I opt to believe it is any explanation other than the first. As I watched this movie I believed that it could not be explained away, like how it was, and it be true. I felt that it would mean the movie didn't have the balls to follow through, which I believe is not what happened, but that the supposed explanation was only meant as an inventation to look deeper. Which subsequently is what is asked of David in this film. He is questioning himself. He is in a coma and has created this dream where he torments himself regarding truth. Who is he? What and who does he value? He recognizes his lack of love from a parent. He recognizes his extreme vanity. He recognizes his struggle to let go of his childish ways and grow up. It's as is quoted and as Jules Verne says, "It's a revolution of the mind." He reconciles his fear of heights as he reconciles his fear of growing up and changing. 

I think I believe this version of the ending because it keeps the film from falling apart in the third act, at least in my opinion.
