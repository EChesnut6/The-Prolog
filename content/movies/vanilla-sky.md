## Primer

#### The Premise

David Aames, a young wealthy publishing tycoon in New York City, appears to have it all, except the right girl. His carefree life is incomplete without someone truly special to share it with, but as he attempts to navigates his lovelife, the rest of  reality starts to cave in.

##### Essentials
*Vanilla Sky* is hard to pigeonhole into one genre, because it just isn't one genre, or even two. The movie has a somewhat drastic tone and genre change, moving from a classic romance to a psychological thriller with a splash of sci-fi mixed in. As a result, the film demands the viewer question what is real while watching. I do suggest watching the [trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k09OX40NLUw) and stopping at **1:22**. From which you'll have gathered the premise and just a touch more without spoiling anything important.

##### Remix
*Vanilla Sky* is actually a remake of the Spanish movie *Abre los ojos* ([[Open Your Eyes]]), which came out in 1997. Cameron Crowe, the director of the former was quoted in an interview where he explained that he considers his version more of a remix rather than a true remake. Alejandro Amenábar, the director of the later, has also praised the work of Crowe, comparing them as,

> "the same song sung by very different voices, one more operatic while the other is rock and roll."
> — Alejandro Amenábar

Further exemplifying the unique connection between the two films is that Penelope Cruz plays the same character, Sofia, in both. This choice was multifaceted as Cruz is a strong actress who carries over her charm from the first flawlessly. However, by casting Cruz, Crowe is able to better convey the unique dialog his film has with the original. As to avoid spoilers and keep from accidental inception, I'll keep my thoughts for the review. 

##### Keep in Mind
Due to the nature of the narative, the film can feel extremely dense, so as a headsup, here is something to keep in mind while watching. Pay special attention to the use of commercial music. Crowe has noted in interviews that the soundtrack was chosen specifically to comment directly on the characters' state as well as the film itself. Several songs are also used in an ironic sense, being turned on their head to juxtapose the current situation. Many of these songs were popular at the time or had been in the past, especially in commercials. Crowe loved the idea of using commercial and popular music in a nonconventional way.

##### From the Future
For viewers like myself who weren't around for the 2001 release, the real-life tabloid drama that was unfolding adds a strange and extra layer of tension and context regarding the romantic scenes. During filming in the year 2000, Tom Cruise was married to Nicole Kidman. It was during this filming that Tom Cruise met Penelope Cruz. In August of 2001, 4 months before *Vanilla Sky* was set to release, Tom Cruise finalized his divorce with Nicole Kidman. It was that same month that his relationhsip with Penelope Cruz went public. Of course the tabloids went wild. "Was their acting out romance what led to Cruise and Kidman's divorce???" While this doesn't make a significant difference watching the film, it does add an extra layer of context which I think is fun if not important. For peace of mind, Cruise and Cruz's highly publicized and scrutinized real-life relationship lasted about 3 years ending due to conflicting and demanding schedules.
