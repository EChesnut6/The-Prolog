## Primer

I don't think there's anything I can really say that will prepare you for this. This might be a movie that can be analyzed for a lifetime and never fully understood. You have to watch it multiple times. I've only just seen it once and feel I need to watch it all again.

I can definitely say this movie is not for many people at all. Even of movie fans, this is probably not meant for many. I can sit and enjoy a slow paced movie and not get bored, but if that sounds like a chore to you, you probably won't love it. I don't even know if I can say that I do love it, but I feel the need to rewatch it, and I'm not dreading doing so...

I think this movie has its die hards who genuinely love studying it and are probably overlapped a lot with the people who read Dostoyevsky, Kafka, and Nietzsche. If you love philosophy you'll probably enjoy this. I'm interested in those writers but don't spend my time reading so I haven't done that. I hope to revisit this one in a month or so and get a better impression of what the movie has to say and what it means to me. 

## Review

The only thing I'm confident about is that this is a analogy for faith. The Stalker is possibly God or maybe Jesus. He demands his followers do as he says. They must follow his path are they'll be damned. When they do stray and end up back with him, they are lucky to be alive. The Room is probably then heavan. I'm not totally sure what a lot of the middle ended up being. The dream sequences I guess were really confusing. I kept googling different types of symbolism which probably distracted me a little too much. I also think I may have been Kubricking this movie a bit (*Kubricking - to overanalyze a movie and draw conclusions based on symbolism and meaning that wasn't put there on purpose by the creator and is either coincidence or complete horseshit*). They don't go into The Room because they don't believe(?) and God (the stalker) gets kinda upset. The child has powers? or its the train. The wife is mother Mary? or God's wife? I'm not sure. The writer put on the crown of thorns so is he Jesus? I know for a fact that Andrei Tarkovsky was a very devout Christian, so I feel like most of what I'm spitballing is correct, but I'm not completely sure. *Now googling the symbolism of a louse*

OH wait, what's up with the dog??? I have no clue what that's supposed to represent. Also, the coloring. We have boring bland reality and the practically (and maybe actually) mythical coloring of the Zone. Also, that implies something extra special about the daughter (Monkey). 

For now I'm giving the enjoyment an 8, while this movie is quite slowly paced, it didn't feel like an awful lot happened, I never found myself bored. I was constantly searching for meaning and appreciating the beautiful cinematography. At the minimum I can say that the pictures on the screen were nice to look at the majority of the time. There were some wild choices and I just have to accept that Tarkovsky was on demon time or something, just trying shit out there like Kobe in the 4th quarter.
